OCTOBER 18, 2026

Can I Trust God With the Real Me?

READ: John 1:12
ALSO READ: Romans 8:14–17 · Romans 8:37–39
DEVOTIONAL

Sometimes we're afraid that if people really knew everything about us, they might not want us anymore.

So we hide.

We hide mistakes.
We hide insecurity.
We hide questions.
We hide pain.

And eventually we can start hiding from God too.

But here's the problem:

God already knows.

And He still invites you closer.

John says that those who receive Jesus are given the right to become children of God.

Not employees.

Not visitors.

Not people trying to earn their way into the family.

Children.

Romans 8 says nothing can separate us from the love of God found in Christ.

That means your worst moment doesn't suddenly surprise God.

You can bring Him the real you.

Your questions.
Your failures.
Your fears.
Your doubts.

God can handle the truth about you because He already knows the truth about you—and still loves you.

THINK

Take a minute.

Is there anything about myself I've been afraid to bring to God?

Why?

PRAY

Talk to God.

Jesus, thank You that I don't have to pretend around You. You know every part of me and still invite me close. Help me believe that my identity is found in being Your child, not in being perfect. Amen.

DO

Live it out.

Tell God something you've been afraid to say out loud.

No polished prayer.

Just honesty.

THE QUESTION THAT FOLLOWS US

Who gets to define me?

Not my past.
Not my mistakes.
Not my friends.
Not social media.
Not my feelings.
Not my circumstances.
GOD DOES.

“Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” — Isaiah 43:1

Can I Trust God With Who I'm Becoming?

READ: 2 Corinthians 5:17
ALSO READ: Galatians 2:20 · Colossians 3:1–4
DEVOTIONAL

One of the biggest lies about identity is:

“This is just who I am.”

Maybe you've heard people say:

I've always had an attitude.
I've always done this.
My family is just like this.
I can't change.

But Jesus doesn't only forgive your past.

Jesus gives you a new life.

Second Corinthians 5:17 says:

If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation.

Following Jesus doesn't mean you'll never struggle again.

It means your struggle no longer gets to tell you who you are.

Your mistake isn't your identity.

Your addiction isn't your identity.

Your anger isn't your identity.

Your past isn't your identity.

Jesus is.

And becoming who God created you to be is often a process.

So don't judge what God is doing in your life by one unfinished chapter.

THINK

Take a minute.

What part of my old identity have I continued carrying even though Jesus says I'm new?

PRAY

Talk to God.

Jesus, help me stop identifying myself by the person I used to be. Thank You for making me new. Give me the strength to walk differently because I belong to You. Keep changing my heart, my thinking, my choices, and my life. Amen.

DO

Live it out.

Finish this sentence:

“Because I belong to Jesus, I don't have to be __________ anymore.”

Then write:

“In Christ, I am becoming __________.”

THE QUESTION THAT FOLLOWS US

Who gets to define me?

Not my past.
Not my mistakes.
Not my friends.
Not social media.
Not my feelings.
Not my circumstances.
GOD DOES.

“Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” — Isaiah 43:1

NOVEMBER 1, 2026

Can I Trust God With My Purpose?

READ: Matthew 5:13–16
ALSO READ: Ephesians 2:10 · 1 Corinthians 6:19–20 · Philippians 3:20
DEVOTIONAL

What if God didn't save you just so you could go to church?

What if He saved you so you could be the Church everywhere you go?

Your school matters.

Your team matters.

Your neighborhood matters.

Your friend group matters.

And God placed you there for a reason.

Jesus told His followers:

“You are the light of the world.”

Notice He didn't say:

You might become light one day.

He said:

You are.

You don't need a microphone to have ministry.

You don't need to be a pastor.

You don't have to know every Bible verse.

Sometimes being sent looks like:

Sitting beside someone who's alone.
Praying for a friend.
Refusing to participate when everybody else is doing wrong.
Inviting somebody to church.
Showing kindness when cruelty would be easier.
Sharing what Jesus has done in your life.

You were created by God.

You were chosen by God.

You were accepted into God's family.

You were made new through Christ.

And now...

YOU ARE SENT.

THINK

Take a minute.

Where has God already placed me that I could begin representing Jesus?

PRAY

Talk to God.

God, use my life. Help me understand that my purpose isn't somewhere far away—it starts wherever You have placed me today. Give me courage to represent Jesus at home, at school, with my friends, and everywhere I go. Amen.

DO

Live it out.

Choose one person this week.

Pray for them every day.

Then look for one opportunity to encourage them, serve them, invite them, or tell them what Jesus means to you.

THE QUESTION THAT FOLLOWS US

Who gets to define me?

Not my past.
Not my mistakes.
Not my friends.
Not social media.
Not my feelings.
Not my circumstances.
GOD DOES.

“Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” — Isaiah 43:1

Can I Trust How God Made Me?

READ: Genesis 1:26–27
ALSO READ: Psalm 139:13–14 · Ephesians 2:10
DEVOTIONAL

Have you ever looked at somebody else and thought:

I wish I looked like them.
I wish I had their personality.
I wish I could do what they can do.
I wish I had their life.

Comparison can make you question something deeper than yourself.

It can make you question the Creator.

Genesis says that when God made humanity, He made us in His image. That means your value didn't begin with your appearance, popularity, athletic ability, followers, grades, or what other people think about you.

Your value began with God.

Psalm 139 says you are fearfully and wonderfully made.

That doesn't mean you'll love everything about yourself every day. It means there is a difference between something you may want to grow in and believing that God made a mistake when He made you.

God doesn't make accidental people.

Ephesians 2:10 calls you God's workmanship.

So here's the question:

If I trust the Creator, can I trust what He created?

You don't have to become someone else to become valuable.

God already gave you value.

THINK

Take a minute.

Who have I been comparing myself to lately?

And an even deeper question:

Who have I allowed to define me?

Friends?
Social media?
My past?
My appearance?
Other people's opinions?

Or God?

PRAY

Talk to God.

God, help me see myself the way You see me. Forgive me for allowing comparison and other people's opinions to become louder than Your voice. Teach me to trust that You were intentional when You created me. Amen.

DO

Live it out.

Every morning this week, look in the mirror and say:

“God made me on purpose, for a purpose.”

THE QUESTION THAT FOLLOWS US

Who gets to define me?

Not my past.
Not my mistakes.
Not my friends.
Not social media.
Not my feelings.
Not my circumstances.
GOD DOES.

“Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” — Isaiah 43:1

Can I Trust That God Really Wants Me?

READ: Isaiah 43:1
ALSO READ: 1 Peter 2:9 · Ephesians 1:3–6
DEVOTIONAL

There is a difference between being allowed in the room and being wanted in the room.

Sometimes people tolerate you.

Sometimes people include you because they have to.

And if you've experienced enough rejection, you can eventually start believing that God feels the same way.

But listen to what God says:

“I have called you by name; you are mine.” — Isaiah 43:1

God doesn't just know that you exist.

He knows your name.

First Peter says you are chosen.

Think about that.

Before somebody overlooked you...
before somebody rejected you...
before somebody unfollowed you...
before somebody decided you weren't enough...

God chose you.

Your identity isn't built on whether everybody wants you.

It is built on the reality that God does.

THINK

Take a minute.

Where have I been trying the hardest to prove that I belong?

What would change if I truly believed:

“I belong to God”?

PRAY

Talk to God.

Father, thank You for choosing me. Help me stop chasing acceptance from people when I already have belonging in You. When rejection hurts, remind me that rejection cannot cancel what You have spoken over my life. I belong to You. Amen.

DO

Live it out.

Write Isaiah 43:1 somewhere you'll see it this week.

When insecurity hits, say:

“He knows me. He called me. I am His.”

THE QUESTION THAT FOLLOWS US

Who gets to define me?

Not my past.
Not my mistakes.
Not my friends.
Not social media.
Not my feelings.
Not my circumstances.
GOD DOES.

“Fear not, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name, you are mine.” — Isaiah 43:1
